Choosing between datacenter and residential proxies is the first decision most people face when they start buying proxies — and picking the wrong type is the most common reason a proxy "doesn't work." Both hide your real IP address, but they come from completely different places, and that difference decides whether a target website trusts them.

This guide explains how the two types differ, what each one is good at, and a simple way to decide which you actually need.

## The one-sentence difference

A **datacenter proxy** is an IP address that belongs to a server hosting company. It lives in a data center, it's fast, and it's cheap — but websites can often tell it's not a regular person.

A **residential proxy** is an IP address assigned by a home internet provider (ISP) to a real household. To a website, traffic through it looks like an ordinary person browsing from home, which makes it much harder to flag.

Everything else — price, speed, and where you should use each — follows from that single distinction.

## Datacenter proxies

Datacenter proxies are generated in bulk on servers, so they're abundant and inexpensive. That's their superpower: you get a lot of bandwidth and very low latency for very little money.

**Strengths**

- **Speed.** Server-grade connections mean fast page loads and high throughput, which matters for scraping or anything bandwidth-heavy.
- **Price.** The cost per IP is far lower than residential, so they scale cheaply.
- **Stability.** The IP doesn't change under you unless you want it to.

**Weaknesses**

- **Easier to detect.** Their IP ranges are publicly known to belong to hosting providers, so strict sites can block them on sight.
- **Shared reputation.** If someone abused the same subnet before you, you can inherit the bad reputation.

**Best for:** sites that don't aggressively fingerprint visitors — SEO rank checking, price monitoring, accessing general public data, testing from different locations, and high-volume scraping of tolerant targets. If a site lets datacenter IPs through, there's no reason to pay more.

## Residential proxies

Residential proxies route your traffic through real home connections, so the destination site sees an ordinary ISP customer. That trust is exactly what you're paying extra for.

**Strengths**

- **Hard to detect.** The IP belongs to a real ISP subscriber, so it blends in with normal traffic.
- **Passes strict sites.** Platforms that block datacenter ranges — social networks, marketplaces, sneaker and ticketing sites, sensitive dashboards — are far more forgiving of residential IPs.
- **Location targeting.** You can appear to browse from a specific country.

**Weaknesses**

- **More expensive.** You're paying for scarcity and trust.
- **Sometimes slower.** Home connections vary, so latency is less predictable than a data center's.

**Best for:** managing multiple accounts, social media automation, ad verification, market research on strict platforms, and anything where getting flagged as a "bot" is the main risk.

## How to decide in 30 seconds

Ask yourself one question: **does my target website block or challenge datacenter IPs?**

- **If no** (or you're not sure and the target is tolerant): start with datacenter proxies. They're cheaper and faster, and you can always upgrade.
- **If yes** (strict social platform, marketplace, or anything tied to a real identity): go residential. Datacenter IPs will just burn through bans.

A practical approach: test with a small datacenter plan first. If you see captchas, soft blocks, or instant bans, switch that workload to residential. Many people run a mix — datacenter for bulk, tolerant work and residential for the few targets that demand it.

## A note on account safety

Whichever type you choose, two habits reduce bans more than the proxy type itself:

- **One identity per IP.** Don't rotate ten accounts through a single IP. If you run many profiles, pair each with its own proxy — this is where an [antidetect browser setup](/blog/how-to-set-up-proxies-in-antidetect-browser) comes in.
- **Match the location.** Keep the proxy's country consistent with the account's history and language settings.

## Where rotating fits in

Some jobs don't need one fixed IP — they need a *fresh* IP on almost every request, such as large-scale scraping. That's a third pattern called rotation, which you can get on top of either type. ## Quick recap

- **Datacenter** = fast and cheap, but easier to detect. Great default for tolerant targets.
- **Residential** = looks like a real person, harder to block. Worth the premium on strict sites.
- Test cheap first, upgrade only the workloads that get flagged.
